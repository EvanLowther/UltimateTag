# Ultimate Tag: guide for AI agents

A simple Roblox tag game with a classic studded look. Code lives in files and syncs into Studio with Rojo.

## Style rules
- **Studded, simple, classic** for the placeholder and test maps: Plastic material, bright solid colors, `TopSurface = Studs` / `BottomSurface = Inlet`.
- **Real maps** (the School) use Roblox's real materials (CeramicTiles, Plaster, WoodPlanks, Metal, Brick...) and lots of non-colliding detail (posters, signs, pipes) to feel lived in. Detail never collides or blocks raycasts (`CanCollide`/`CanQuery`/`CanTouch` off), so wall runs and the camera ignore it; only deliberate props are solid.
- **The lobby** is vibrant, flat-shaded low-poly (meshes from Blender with one palette texture), matching the reference art.
- **Lighting** (in `default.project.json`, Rojo-managed) is bright and saturated: ShadowMap, a ColorCorrection boost, soft Bloom, light Atmosphere. Simple white speed trails are the only other effect.
- **UI** (every menu, popup and HUD piece) uses the "Street sprint" style in `client/Menu/Ui.luau`: flat black panels with cut corners (`Ui.panel`), slanted bars, tabs and buttons (`Ui.slab`, `Ui.button`, `Ui.tabs`), hazard yellow, hot pink, cyan and lime, Bangers headlines (`Ui.title`) over heavy Titillium Web capitals (`Ui.caps`), slanted progress bars and XP segments, rarity strips (`Ui.rarityStrip`; Iridescent shimmers with `Ui.holo`), a coin pill and a column of slanted page buttons on the left, the level bar at the bottom center. Each window has its own header color (Shop yellow, Locker cyan, Quests pink, Rewards lime, Leaders orange). Build new UI from these helpers, not raw instances. (The dev panel keeps its own plain look.) **No emoji anywhere** (menus, HUD or world effects): icons are drawn in code by `client/Icons` (`Ui.icon`, `Ui.centerIcon`, `Ui.iconText`, a button's `icon`; in BillboardGuis `Icons.draw`), from flat shapes in a main color and an accent. A new icon is a new entry in its `DRAW` table, built from its shape helpers. Gotchas: upper-case with `Ui.upper`, never `string.upper` (it mangles accented names in Roblox); a frame with AutomaticSize can't hold Scale-sized children such as a slab's pieces (they stretch it to fill its parent), so size it with `Ui.fitWidth`; keep an icon's frame square (its shapes are sized against both sides, so turned shapes skew in a wide frame); give icons on the dark wells a light accent (a dark one vanishes).
- Keep it simple. Prefer one small module per feature over big scripts.

## Maps
Each map is a folder in `src/maps/<MapName>/`:
- `init.meta.json` makes it a `Model` with attributes `DisplayName`, `MinPlayers`, `MaxPlayers` (the player counts it suits: its size tier) and `InRotation` (false keeps it out of games).
- Optional map attributes: `OutOfBounds` (for `tools/mapcheck.py`, see the geometry rules below), `KillHeight` (world Y: falling below it kills you, and you respawn on the map's spawns; server/MapRespawn) and `Lighting` (a preset in `Config.MapLighting.Presets`, like `Night`, shown while your camera is near the map; client/MapLighting). Gadgets are attributes on parts: `LaunchVelocity` (Vector3, a launch pad), `SpeedBoost` (number, studs/s when you jump or run off it) and `Slip` (number, seconds you're down after slipping on it: you fall back and skid on with your momentum; no map uses it now); client/MapGadgets.
- Pieces are `*.model.json` files, one per part or model, in the studded plastic style. Don't put a `name` field in a top-level model file (the file name is the name).
- A `Spawns/` folder of invisible, non-colliding, anchored parts: where players are placed at the start of a round. Have at least as many as the map's `MaxPlayers` if you can (they're reused if not).
- Maps are built around the origin (the lobby is far away at X=2000).
- Place parts with `CFrame`, never `Position`: `"CFrame": { "CFrame": { "position": [x, y, z], "orientation": [[1,0,0],[0,1,0],[0,0,1]] } }`. `Position` only works during live sync and is lost in the built `.rbxlx`, so everything loads at the origin.
- To add a map, copy a placeholder folder, change its attributes and pieces. MapService picks it up automatically.

- Any part with the `SlideBlocker` attribute blocks standing players but not sliding or crouching ones (CollisionGroups also handles maps loaded later).
- **Ladders** are how players get up anything taller than a jump (about 5.5; there's no wall climb or mantle), used sparingly (the School has one): Roblox characters climb TrussParts by themselves, so `maplib.ladder(...)` stands rails and rungs flat on a face (`gap` 0.07 off it; more on a curved wall, whose flat pieces bow in) with invisible trusses (2 x 2, tops level with the ledge) whose fronts are level with the rails and the rest sunk into the wall, and `client/Ladders` holds `MoveState.busy = "Climb"` while you're on one so no other move fires mid-climb. A ladder needs a solid backing at least 2 deep for its whole height (a wall, or a column under a floor's edge: never standing free in a room, which floats), nothing solid in front of it, and an opening at the top (split any railing).
- **Geometry rules (no flicker, no clipping)**, checked by `tools/mapcheck.py` for every map in rotation and by each generator before it writes: no two visible faces in the same plane facing the same way where they overlap (z-fighting: they flicker as the camera moves), and nothing laid less than 0.04 over a surface. So: build stacked steps (bleachers, tiers) as side-by-side blocks, not overlapping ones; walls end at the face of the wall they meet (not its center line); floor finishes and overlays stop at wall faces; decoration sits at least 0.05 off its surface (more off curved walls, whose flat pieces bow in); a part never tops out exactly at a floor's surface unless the floor sits on it; round floors and walls come from `maplib`'s exact pieces (`ring_segment`, `ring_wall`, `ring_floor`), never overlapping boxes; window sill strips sit on the wall's faces; nothing (decoration included) cuts into or sits right in front of window glass; a ladder's invisible truss is sunk into something solid (a floating ladder is flagged). It also checks reach: every walkable area can be reached from the spawns and you can always get back (walking, jumps, drops, crouching under, ladders, launch pads, level swinging platforms; wall runs passed in as routes). Tops that aren't play area (roofs, ceilings, tall furniture) go in the map's `OutOfBounds` attribute (comma-separated part names).

## Lobby
The lobby is a low-poly tropical island made in Blender (`tools/blender/make_lobby.py`) and imported into Studio once. After that, **Studio owns it**: it lives in the place file (save the place, and publish to keep it safe), you edit it freely in Studio, and Rojo never touches `Workspace.Lobby`.
- Layout: `Workspace.Lobby/<Category>/<Object>` Models (Island, Water, Horizon, Nature, Props, Landmarks, Lounge_Zone, UI_Assets, Shop_Structure, Lighthouse), each with its pivot at its base, plus `LobbySpawn` and an invisible `Boundary` ring. One mesh per object (palette texture), `*_Glow` parts are Neon, decorations don't collide, palms have invisible trunk colliders.
- Setting up (or redoing) the lobby: run the generator from the repo root (writes `assets_src/lobby/UltimateTagLobby.fbx` and `src/tools/LobbyData.luau`; run `stylua src` afterwards), then in Studio **Import 3D** the FBX and run `require(game.ServerStorage.Tools.SetupLobby)()` in the command bar, then save. SetupLobby fixes the import's scale/rotation/position with four marker cubes (island center goes to `Config.Lobby.Position`), organizes it, sets collision, and adds sign text (SurfaceGuis), Seats, prompts, the leaderboard screen and the wheel's spin data. Re-running on a new import replaces the old lobby (manual Studio edits are lost then).
- Working parts find their pieces by CollectionService tag, so they keep working when moved: `LobbyLeaderboard` (screen part), `RewardWheel` (wheel part, with `SpinAxis`/`SpinCenter`/`Segments`/`Angle` attributes in its own space), `RewardWheelPrompt`, `AvatarShopPrompt` (shows its `Message` attribute).

## Art pipeline (Blender)
Blender 5 is installed (`blender` on the PATH). Generator scripts in `tools/blender/` build models in code; run them with `blender --background --python <script> -- <out_dir> [preview_dir]`.
- Map generators (`tools/*_layout.py`) write Rojo map folders; `tools/maplib.py` has the shared helpers (parts, blocks, cylinders, flat triangles, straight walls with doors and windows, slabs with holes, round walls and floors from exact pieces, ladders, parts on a round wall, lights, text, particles, writing the folder) and `tools/mapcheck.py` the checks every map must pass (flicker, windows, ladders, reach). Designs not in the game yet live in `tools/designs/` (`rooftops_v1.py`, the Rooftop District; writes its map only with `--write`), and `tools/designs/canvas_export.py` exports one for its 3D design canvas.
- `make_lobby.py`: the lobby island (see Lobby). 118 objects, one mesh each (about 36k triangles total), colored by a one-row palette texture (`LobbyPalette.png`, flip-proof); each object has its own mesh named after it (so imported pieces are named predictably); glow pieces separate; sign text is data, not mesh. Studs, Z up, front faces -Y. Writes `assets_src/lobby/UltimateTagLobby.blend`/`.fbx`, `src/tools/LobbyData.luau`, and with a preview dir, renders (`assets_src/lobby/previews/`). Also runs from Blender's Text Editor.
- `make_character.py`, `smooth_body.py`: earlier character experiments (unused).

export PATH="$HOME/.rokit/bin:$PATH"

## Features
**Game flow (0.26.0).** `Config.Game.Mode = "Elimination"`: players wait in the lobby; with `MinPlayers` (Studio: `StudioMinPlayers`) a `LobbyCountdown` starts, then a game. Each round MapService picks a map for the number of players still in, everyone is placed on its spawn points, the game mode assigns roles, everyone is frozen for `IntroTime` (Its for `ItHeadStart` longer), then the `RoundTime` timer runs. When it ends the mode decides who is eliminated; they go back to the lobby and spectate (Q/E to switch). Rounds continue on new maps until one player is left: they win (+1 Win) and everyone returns to the lobby. Only It can tag, by left-clicking (a swipe) within `Config.Tag.Range`, and only while a round is Playing. Test with 2+ players in Studio (Test > Clients and Servers > Local Server).
- Game modes live in `src/server/Modes/`. Each has `Name`, `setup(players) -> status text` and `finish(players) -> eliminated`. The first is **Hot Potato** (`Modes/HotPotato.luau`): `EliminateFraction` (about 45%) of players start as It, tagging passes It, whoever is It when time runs out is eliminated. To add a mode, add a module with that shape and point `GameLoop`'s `mode` at it.
- `Config.Game.Mode = "Sandbox"`: the old free-play test mode on TestGround (one It, nothing ends) for movement testing. The dev panel switches modes while the server runs (`GameLoop.setMode`).

## Progression and rewards
Coins and XP come from playing (games, rounds survived, tags, wins, minutes played, and moves during rounds: `Config.Progression`). Levels pay coins, a free crate every 5 levels and exclusive cosmetics at set levels. Cosmetics (trails, tag effects, titles, victories; `shared/Catalog`) come from the daily shop (changes at 00:00 UTC; never Iridescent), the Mystery crate (bought in the Shop, opened in Rewards; odds shown, duplicates possible), levels, the login streak and achievements. Rarity sets the look: Common one color, Rare two colors or a simple pattern, Epic a gradient, Legendary a gradient plus effects, Iridescent shifting holo colors plus something special (afterimages, Shatter, a framed title with its serial number, a holo statue), with a server-wide shout-out when one is unboxed. Victories are Epic and up. Exclusive (green) items are only earned. Daily and weekly quests, achievements and the 7-day streak track themselves and are claimed by hand. Everything is saved in one profile per player (server/PlayerData). Test with the dev panel's **Progress** tab: give coins/XP/crates, unlock everything, finish quests, and move the calendar forward a day or week. Saving needs API access in Studio (Game Settings > Security); without it everything works for the session.

Rules of thumb:
- Anything a player earns goes through `Progression.record` (events) or `Progression.grant` (reward bundles), never by editing coins directly, so popups, quests and achievements all see it.
- Testing tools go in the dev panel: a new server command is a handler in `server/DevCommands.luau` (it already checks access), a new tunable is a line in `Config.DevPanel.Tunables`.
- Server owns all game state. Clients read attributes and ask the server through RemoteEvents in `shared/Remotes.luau` (add new names to its list).
